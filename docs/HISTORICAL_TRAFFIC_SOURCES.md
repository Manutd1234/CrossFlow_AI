## Acquired free historical evidence

**Batam, 16–18 February 2023:** Kurniawan and Anwar's [Sigma Teknika paper](https://www.journal.unrika.ac.id/index.php/sigmateknika/article/view/5642), DOI `10.33373/sigmateknika.v6i2.5642`, contains measured speeds in Table 11, printed page 487 / PDF page 9. The [transcribed CSV](../backend/data/research/batam-gajah-mada-2023.csv) contains **48 direction/half-hour observations** from a 100 m section of Jalan Gajah Mada near Kemilau Bangsa School: 07:00–09:00 and 16:00–18:00 on three dates, both published directions. Reported speeds span **20.35–34.32 km/h**. The [manifest](../backend/data/research/batam-gajah-mada-2023.manifest.json) preserves attribution, source and CSV checksums, CC BY-SA 4.0 licence, source method and limitations. PDF/table transcription was visually checked. The original PDF is retained locally under ignored `backend/data/traffic-imports/batam-survey-2023/`.

The 50.61 km/h reference in that paper is a PKJI calculation, not measured free flow. Original raw samples/counts are unavailable. One reported speed/travel-time cross-check differs by more than 0.5 km/h; it is flagged and retained, not silently corrected. Exact survey coordinates and a mapping to the five app corridors remain unverified. These sparse peak-only observations are visible as research evidence; they are **not silently imported into canonical live history or used to promote a network model**.

**Singapore, 8 March–3 April 2021:** the [SUTD student research archive](https://github.com/EYLeong/traffic_prediction) publishes inert numeric/JSON data containing **53 road links × 7,497 snapshots = 397,341 ordinal speed-band values**, across 27 calendar dates. The source array, road coordinates, timestamps, inspected feature-extraction semantics and SHA-256 hashes are retained locally under ignored `backend/data/traffic-imports/singapore-sutd/`. Publisher code and pickle files were not executed. Source clocks have **no stated timezone**, so chronology is retained without asserting UTC or SGT. Bands 1–8 are not exact speeds; the top band is open-ended.

Contains information from LTA Traffic Speed Bands accessed on 23 October 2026 through that research archive, under the [Singapore Open Data Licence 1.0](https://datamall.lta.gov.sg/content/datamall/en/SingaporeOpenDataLicence.html). No official status or endorsement is implied. The publisher's inspected preprocessing directly copies SpeedBand values, but original LTA response payloads were not available for independent revalidation. No explicit code licence was found in that repository; its code was not reused.

The [independent chronological backtest](verification/singapore-academic-backtest.json) uses **zero synthetic training rows**. It reports all 53 roads individually and compares a bounded Random Forest against persistence and a previous-day baseline. The [runner](../scripts/backtest_academic_singapore.py) validates input checksums, refuses pickle, excludes gaps rather than interpolating, and purges the final forecast labels before later partition origins. ## Additional Batam academic acquisition — second review

The renewed search acquired **28 more dated Batam historical traffic records**, from Bangun, Agustina and Kusmawan (2024), [DOI 10.33373/sigmateknika.v7i2.6939](https://www.journal.unrika.ac.id/index.php/sigmateknika/article/view/6939). Table 4.3, printed p445 / PDF p7, gives four hourly degree-of-saturation ratios per day across **12–18 June 2023** at the Tengku Sulung–Raja M. Saleh unsignalised intersection. The conclusion explicitly supplies that date range, and the Saturday peak is independently dated 17 June 2023 in the text. The [CSV](../backend/data/research/batam-tengku-sulung-2023.csv) and [manifest](../backend/data/research/batam-tengku-sulung-2023.manifest.json) retain the primary source, checksums, CC BY-SA 4.0 attribution and limitations. The source table was rendered and visually checked. Values range 0.716–1.294; these are **derived volume/capacity ratios, not speeds, speed ratios or the app's 0–100 speed-based congestion index**. They contribute **zero canonical speed-training labels**. They are inspectable in Analytics → History with a seven-date filter.

A second acquired [Batam navigation paper](https://www.iieta.org/journals/ijcmem/paper/10.18280/ijcmem.130208), Hernando et al. (2025), DOI `10.18280/ijcmem.130208`, CC BY 4.0, gives **six area-level speed ranges** in Table 1 p314, referring to January–December 2024. The [ranges CSV](../backend/data/research/batam-published-area-speed-ranges-2024.csv) preserves ranges and leaves `exact_observed_at` blank; [its manifest](../backend/data/research/batam-published-area-speed-ranges-2024.manifest.json) records provenance. No dated raw segment archive is published there. Turning each range into a midpoint or repeating it across invented dates would manufacture training labels, so these are excluded from chronological speed-model fitting.

The newer Gajah Mada study, [Islami, Sari and Prastijo (2026)](https://jrs.ft.unand.ac.id/index.php/jrs/article/view/1459), DOI `10.25077/jrs.22.2.204-215.2026`, was also acquired. Its PDF contains capacity, vehicle-class totals and peak saturation calculations, but no verified calendar-dated speed table. It does not supply the missing network time series. The [Bunga Raya–Raden Patah study](https://jurnal.ptdisttd.ac.id/index.php/jpsttd/article/view/555), DOI `10.55511/jpsttd.v11i2.555`, reports Vissim network outcomes; simulated before/after outputs are not measured historical speed labels. The ITERA Suprapto repository and another journal PDF returned access errors; no unavailable contents were treated as acquired data.

Original downloaded PDFs, extracted text and HTTP/hash audits remain under ignored `backend/data/traffic-imports/batam-additional-research/`. No provider purchase, quote request or account creation was performed. These additions improve source coverage but still do **not** establish a free, dated, direction-matched speed archive for all five app corridors. The promotion criteria remain unchanged; sparse or differently measured records are not a reason to relax them.

Request **at least seven consecutive completed local days, preferably four weeks**, with every 15-minute dated epoch across all hours, weekdays and weekends. Start with the matched segments around these five points, retaining each travel direction separately. Choose actual available dates in the portal and record the query, filters, vehicle/probe category, map version and export time. Coverage gaps remain gaps; seven calendar days alone do not establish sufficient usable training/evaluation samples.

Retain the following provider fields and semantics:

- **Time:** original `DATE-TIME`, epoch length and timezone. HERE's field is the **start of the epoch in local time**, not the end or a Unix timestamp. Interpret Batam as `Asia/Jakarta` (WIB, UTC+7), preserve the original, and store an offset-aware UTC conversion. A 15-minute aggregate cannot be treated as fully known at its interval start; preserve interval end/availability for chronological evaluation. [Date/time definition](https://docs.here.com/traffic-analytics/docs/date-date-time)
- **Road and direction:** original `LINK-DIR` or TMC, map version, geometry/bearing and segment length. Build a reviewed crosswalk to CrossFlow corridor IDs with snap distance and direction checks. Do not silently select a parallel road or mix opposing directions. HERE link/TMC direction coding and map-version compatibility must be preserved. [Segment references](https://docs.here.com/traffic-analytics/docs/road-segment-reference-tmc-or-link-dir)
- **Speed and reference:** retain the chosen mean-speed statistic and units. `FREEFLOW` is a generated provider reference: usually the 80th percentile of weekend overnight probes across several years, otherwise map-attribute based. It is **not the original measurement for that epoch**. Keep that provenance separate from observed mean speeds. [Free-flow definition](https://docs.here.com/traffic-analytics/docs/free-flow-speed-freeflow)
- **Evidence and gaps:** request `COUNT`, `CONFIDENCE` and gap-fill status. **Turn gap filling off; reject `GAPFILL=Y` if present.** Missing epochs must not be interpolated and labelled observed. Counts describe sampled observations, not total traffic volume; 15-minute per-vehicle counts may repeat vehicles across constituent five-minute buckets. Preserve raw confidence categories rather than interpreting them as probabilities or TomTom confidence values. [Gap-fill flags](https://docs.here.com/traffic-analytics/docs/gap-filling-gapfill), [sample counts](https://docs.here.com/traffic-analytics/docs/sample-count-count), [confidence categories](https://docs.here.com/traffic-analytics/docs/confidence-confidence)

## Acceptance before import or training

Retain the applicable licence/order and permitted-use evidence alongside a manifest containing source, query ID, retrieval time, original date bounds/timezone, units, field definitions, map/crosswalk version and file hashes. Confirm storage/deletion periods, attribution, permitted users, ML training/evaluation, derived-model retention and redistribution rights. Registration or a successful download does not settle these rights.

Validate a small export first: Batam location and direction, real dated observations, reference-speed provenance, duplicates, invalid/nonfinite or negative actual speeds, nonpositive/invalid free-flow references, sample counts and gap-filled rows. **Preserve valid zero actual speed: stopped traffic is a real observation.** Produce per-corridor/direction/day coverage and missing-epoch reports. A reviewed provider adapter is required before import; HERE identifiers, confidence categories and interval timestamps must not be silently treated as the existing TomTom point format.

After a licence-approved import, run the existing observed chronological backtest with disjoint fit/calibration/holdout periods and its persistence baseline and activation gates. Keep the current serving model's actual provenance visible until a real candidate passes those gates. Do not substitute typical profiles, synthetic replay, another city's data, or trial-registration success for observed Batam evaluation evidence.
